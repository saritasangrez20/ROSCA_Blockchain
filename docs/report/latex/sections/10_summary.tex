\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

Trustless ROSCA replaces the committee organizer's control of money with code. Contributions and
deposits sit in contracts; deposits always cover 100\% of what a paid member still owes, so a member who
takes the pot and disappears is paid for out of their own deposit; the payout order comes from a commit
and reveal draw that no participant can steer; and every payment is a public event. Seven contracts,
four off chain services and a React app implement eight workflows across a five state lifecycle, with
off chain data (names, terms, history, reminders) kept outside the trust path.

In the final stage the system was measured, optimized and attacked. Four optimizations (EIP 1167 clones,
storage packing with immutable module addresses, a paid bitmap per round and events only reputation
history) cut the gas of a whole 4 member circle from 9,619,893 to 4,521,109 ($-$53.0\%), creating a
circle by 87.8\% and settling a round by 40 to 59\%, without changing any function, output or event the
frontend and indexer use. Nine attack scenarios with 38 tests found three real problems in the handed
over code: an admin pause could push every member into default, the last revealer could choose its
payout slot, and the deployer kept a role that could frame members. All three are fixed and proved by
tests that failed before and pass now; the remaining risks are stated openly. The full suite of 124 tests
passes, and the whole stack was checked live.
